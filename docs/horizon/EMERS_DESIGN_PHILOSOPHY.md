# EMERS Design Philosophy

## 1. Purpose

Define how EMERS Trade should look, feel, and behave before implementation.

## 2. Foundational experience principle

EMERS should feel calm when markets are noisy, clear when information is complex, and cautious when consequences are material.

## 3. Design values

Clarity, discipline, restraint, precision, transparency, accessibility, evidence, and user control.

## 4. Calm design

The product should be structured, factual, non-gamified, and non-urgent unless a real operational condition requires urgency.

## 5. Risk-first design

Risk appears before return, exposure before excitement, rejection before workaround, and uncertainty before confidence.

## 6. Human-control design

Consequential actions require explicit user approval. The interface must not preselect or obscure approval choices.

## 7. Explainability

Recommendations must show principal reasons, source type, assumptions, and limitations before action.

## 8. Transparency

The user should always know mode, broker status, data freshness, unresolved issues, and current versus future capability.

## 9. Information density

Dense financial information is acceptable when hierarchy and labels make it understandable. Simplicity must not conceal complexity.

## 10. Progressive disclosure

Show essential status first, then detail, evidence, and diagnostics on demand.

## 11. Consequence visibility

Before confirmation, show instrument, side, order type, quantity, capital, stop, target, maximum planned loss, exposure, broker, and environment.

## 12. State visibility

Every material workflow should display draft, pending, approved, submitted, acknowledged, filled, cancelled, rejected, unresolved, or degraded state.

## 13. Reversibility

Cancellation, rollback, disable, and emergency stop should be visible when applicable.

## 14. Professional character

EMERS should be modern and approachable without being casual, sensational, or profit-obsessed.

## 15. Emotional discipline

No interaction should pressure the user to trade. The product should normalize not acting when evidence or risk does not justify action.

## 16. Prohibited patterns

Prohibited patterns include casino-style presentation, gamified trade frequency, confetti, streaks, flashing profit indicators, fear-of-missing-out language, arbitrary countdown timers, hidden risks, implied certainty, ambiguous submission status, automatic selection of consequential approvals, dark patterns, interfaces that make cancellation difficult, and interfaces that celebrate returns while hiding exposure or drawdown.

## 17. Design-review questions

Does the design show risk first? Does it preserve human control? Is broker state explicit? Is uncertainty visible? Can failure be detected? Can the action be audited? Could a user mistakenly believe a future feature exists today?

## 18. Founding design commitment

EMERS should feel calm when markets are noisy, clear when information is complex, and cautious when consequences are material.
