## 2. Purpose

Project Horizon is a product-design blueprint that converts EMERS principles, product strategy, and architecture into a calm, understandable, safe, and auditable user experience.

## 3. Relationship to the EMERS Constitution

Horizon applies the constitution to interface behavior: safety before speed, risk before return, human judgment, transparent uncertainty, auditability, privacy, and release discipline.

## 4. Relationship to Project Atlas

Atlas defines the ecosystem. Horizon defines what the user sees, how information is organized, and how product states should feel before implementation begins.

## 5. Experience proposition

We are not in the business of predicting markets. We are in the business of helping people make better decisions. EMERS Trade should create clarity before action.

## 6. Current starting point

The current product foundation is v4.0 Stable plus v4.1 planning: deterministic Paper workflows, operator approval, broker-state reconciliation, evidence governance, Atlas, and EMERS vision documents.

## 7. Horizon workstreams

- Information architecture.
- Design philosophy.
- Design-system foundation.
- Dashboard blueprint.
- Screen catalog.
- Core user flows.
- Risk communication.
- Approval and broker-status experience.
- Alerts, failure, mobile, accessibility, microcopy, metrics, and prototypes.

## 8. Design constraints

No visual identity is final. No frontend framework is selected. Current and future capabilities must be clearly separated. No design may imply live trading, guaranteed profit, broker success without acknowledgment, or AI certainty.

## 9. Non-goals

Horizon does not authorize frontend implementation, change v4.1 engineering priorities, finalize branding, implement EMERS Score, select fonts/colors/logos, authorize mobile order submission, or approve commercial launch.

## 10. Decision principles

Clarity before action, risk before reward, calm before urgency, consequence before confirmation, broker truth before assumption, and evidence after material action.

## 11. Required outputs

Required outputs are the Horizon documentation set, decision register, screen catalog, flow definitions, risk standard, approval standard, and prototype plan.

## 12. Validation approach

Future validation should include user review, technical feasibility review, accessibility review, security review, broker-state scenario testing, and prototype walkthroughs with simulated data only.

## 13. Risks

Risks include overdesign, premature visual decisions, hidden risk, unclear broker state, misleading AI language, mobile oversimplification, and allowing prototypes to imply implementation approval.

## 14. Governance

Unresolved design decisions remain in the Horizon Decision Register. No unresolved design choice should be marked approved without evidence.

## 15. Immediate next steps

The next approved implementation item remains V41-PQ-001: Design Paper qualification state machine. Horizon may inform later UX work after separate approval.
