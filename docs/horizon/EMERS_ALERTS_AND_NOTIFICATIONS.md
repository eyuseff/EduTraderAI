# EMERS Alerts and Notifications

## 1. Purpose

Define alert behavior without creating urgency for its own sake.

## 2. Alert philosophy

Alerts exist to protect attention and safety, not to increase trading frequency.

## 3. Severity model

Informational, review required, important, urgent, critical. Urgency must reflect real consequences.

## 4. Alert categories

Risk, broker, data quality, system, security, approval, evidence, and portfolio review.

## 5. Notification channels

Future channels may include in-app, email, mobile push, and secure messaging. No channel is selected here.

## 6. Timing

Timing should reflect consequence, market context, and user quiet periods.

## 7. Deduplication

Alerts should deduplicate repeated state and avoid notification floods.

## 8. Escalation

Escalation should occur only when unresolved state becomes more serious.

## 9. Acknowledgement

Acknowledgement should record user visibility but not imply resolution.

## 10. Resolution

Resolution requires actual state change or documented dismissal.

## 11. Quiet periods

Quiet periods may reduce non-critical alerts but must not hide critical unresolved broker or security states.

## 12. Mobile notifications

Mobile notifications should support monitoring and acknowledgement before approval is separately validated.

## 13. Security notifications

Credential, login, revocation, and unusual access events require clear notification.

## 14. Broker notifications

Broker rejection, fill, cancellation, unresolved state, or failed reconciliation may require alerts.

## 15. Risk notifications

Risk budget, concentration, stale data, and policy violations may require alerts.

## 16. Data-quality notifications

Stale or incomplete data should be visible when material.

## 17. System notifications

System degraded mode, event-delivery failure, or storage failure should be visible.

## 18. Evidence

Material alerts should be auditable.

## 19. Prohibited notification behavior

No marketing-style urgency, repeated pressure to trade, artificial scarcity, profit-only alerts, overload, alerts without resolution path, or hiding unresolved critical alerts.

## 20. Open decisions

Open decisions include channels, quiet-hour defaults, escalation timing, and mobile policy.
