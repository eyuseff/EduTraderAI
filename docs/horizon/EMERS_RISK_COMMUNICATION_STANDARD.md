# EMERS Risk Communication Standard

## 1. Purpose

Set the standard for communicating risk before expected return.

## 2. Risk communication principles

Risk must be visible, plain, contextual, and shown before reward. Uncertainty must remain visible.

## 3. Required risk concepts

Maximum planned loss, position size, capital committed, stop distance, expected reward, reward-to-risk ratio, portfolio exposure after action, concentration after action, correlation or overlapping exposure, liquidity limitations, gap risk, event risk, assumption sensitivity, stale-data risk, model uncertainty, and broker-execution uncertainty.

## 4. Risk hierarchy

Portfolio risk, position risk, execution risk, data risk, model risk, scenario risk, then expected reward.

## 5. Position risk

Show position size, stop distance, planned loss, capital committed, and thesis risk.

## 6. Portfolio risk

Show exposure after action, risk budget, concentration, and overlapping exposure.

## 7. Concentration

Concentration must be named when a new trade increases dependence on one symbol, sector, factor, or correlated exposure.

## 8. Liquidity

Liquidity limits and spread concerns should be visible where material.

## 9. Market risk

Market regime and volatility are context, not certainty.

## 10. Event risk

Earnings, macro events, and known catalysts should be shown as uncertainty sources.

## 11. Data risk

Stale, missing, delayed, or incomplete data must be labeled.

## 12. Model risk

Model output must be framed as interpretation, not truth.

## 13. Broker and execution risk

Show order type, fill risk, cancellation uncertainty, partial fill risk, and unresolved broker state.

## 14. Scenario presentation

Scenarios must distinguish estimates from facts and should show downside cases.

## 15. Uncertainty

Distinguish known fact, calculated amount, configured rule, estimate, scenario, model interpretation, and unresolved uncertainty.

## 16. Warnings

Warnings should explain consequence and safest next action.

## 17. Risk acknowledgement

Acknowledgement should be explicit when risk is material.

## 18. Visual requirements

Risk cannot rely on color alone. Text, symbols, units, timestamps, and labels are required.

## 19. Prohibited communication

Avoid: safe trade, guaranteed setup, certain outcome, cannot lose, low risk without explanation, AI-confirmed winner.

## 20. Validation criteria

Users should be able to explain planned loss, exposure, uncertainty, broker state, and why the platform approved or rejected a plan.
