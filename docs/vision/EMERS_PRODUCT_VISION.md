## 2. Executive summary

EMERS Trade is an intelligent trading decision and execution platform designed
to help users identify opportunities, examine risk, approve decisions, interact
with supported brokers, and retain a transparent audit trail.

The product promise is simple: EMERS Trade helps users make more disciplined and
informed investment decisions without surrendering control.

## 3. Product identity

EMERS Trade is the emerging customer-facing product identity. EduTraderAI remains
the existing technical engine. A likely presentation is:

EMERS Trade

Powered by EduTraderAI

This naming is working language and must not be treated as legally cleared or
commercially final.

## 4. Problem statement

Many trading tools emphasize speed, excitement, prediction, or simplified
signals without giving users enough visibility into downside, assumptions,
execution state, and evidence. EMERS Trade addresses the need for disciplined,
explainable, broker-aware decision support.

## 5. Target users

The initial target user is a private, sophisticated individual investor who
values disciplined risk management, explainable recommendations, operator
approval, and reliable broker integration.

Potential future segments may include:

- Experienced individual investors.
- Active traders seeking stronger risk controls.
- Family investment offices.
- Small professional investment teams.
- Advisers using decision-support tools.
- Users managing portfolios across supported brokers.

All multi-user and professional segments are future possibilities requiring
legal, regulatory, security, and product validation.

## 6. User needs

Users need to understand opportunities, downside, exposure, recommendation
rationale, broker readiness, approval requirements, and the final status of any
submitted order. They also need clear limitations, safe defaults, and evidence
that material actions can be reconstructed.

## 7. Product promise

EMERS Trade helps users make more disciplined and informed investment decisions
without surrendering control.

## 8. Product experience

The intended product flow is:

1. Observe markets.
2. Identify candidates.
3. Understand the evidence.
4. Evaluate downside and exposure.
5. Compare possible outcomes.
6. Review recommendation rationale.
7. Approve, reject, or modify the action.
8. Submit only through an authorized broker workflow.
9. Monitor broker acknowledgment and status.
10. Reconcile the result.
11. Preserve an audit record.
12. Learn from completed decisions.

## 9. Core capabilities

Core and future capabilities include:

- Market scanning.
- Signal and candidate ranking.
- Explainable decision support.
- Position sizing.
- Risk and reward analysis.
- Scenario evaluation.
- Explicit approval workflow.
- Paper broker integration.
- Order-state monitoring.
- Portfolio awareness.
- Audit and evidence records.
- Performance analytics.
- Alerts and notifications.
- Future web and mobile access.
- Future supported multi-broker architecture.

Future items should not be implied as current capabilities until built and
validated.

## 10. Differentiation

EMERS Trade should be differentiated by disciplined risk rather than excitement,
explainability rather than opaque signals, evidence rather than unsupported
claims, operator control rather than uncontrolled automation, broker-state
reconciliation rather than assumed success, release governance and auditability,
safety-first defaults, and professional capabilities with understandable
presentation.

No direct competitor comparison should be made without separate market research.

## 11. Initial operating model

The initial operating model is:

- Private single-user deployment.
- Single application process.
- Paper-first qualification.
- Supported broker APIs only.
- No implicit live-trading capability.
- Local audit trail.
- Controlled operator approval.
- Deployment restrictions remain until explicitly removed through validation.

## 12. Commercial product direction

Long-term direction may include secure web access, mobile monitoring and
approvals, cloud-based API services, multi-user identity and access management,
supported multi-broker adapters, portfolio-level analytics, explainable AI
assistance, configurable risk policies, alerts and notifications,
institutional-grade auditability, and evidence-based release qualification.

These capabilities are directional, not current commitments.

## 13. Product boundaries

EMERS Trade must not be described as:

- A guaranteed-profit system.
- An autonomous money-making machine.
- A replacement for professional financial advice.
- A platform that eliminates market risk.

EMERS is decision-support technology, risk-analysis technology, controlled
execution infrastructure, portfolio and market intelligence, and an auditable
trading platform. It is not automatically a registered investment adviser,
broker, exchange, bank, fund manager, or guarantor of investment performance.
Future commercial operation may require specialist legal and regulatory analysis
in every relevant jurisdiction.

## 14. Success measures

Success should be measured by decision clarity, user understanding of risk,
successful broker-state reconciliation, reduced unsafe configurations,
auditable material actions, test quality, evidence quality, release reliability,
and user trust earned through honest limitations.

## 15. Strategic roadmap

The near-term roadmap remains v4.1 safety work: deterministic Paper
qualification, coordination analysis, event-publisher observability, performance
thresholds, and release automation. Public commercialization is outside the
immediate v4.1 implementation scope unless separately approved.

## 16. Relationship to EduTraderAI

EduTraderAI remains the technical engine and current source-module identity.
EMERS Trade is the emerging user-facing product identity. Source modules should
not be renamed without controlled, backward-compatible migration planning.

## 17. Open strategic questions

- What legal and regulatory obligations apply in each target jurisdiction?
- Which broker integrations should be supported after Alpaca Paper qualification
matures?
- What evidence schema should become durable for commercial operations?
- How should EMERS present AI assistance without overstating certainty?
