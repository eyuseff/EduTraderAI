# EMERS Experience Blueprint

## 1. Experience vision

EMERS Trade should give the user clarity before action. The platform should help the user understand what matters, what changed, what requires attention, what risk is already being carried, what uncertainty remains, and whether any action is actually necessary.

The first screen must not pressure the user to trade. It should first communicate portfolio condition, risk exposure, important changes, unresolved issues, and whether action is required.

## 2. User promise

EMERS Trade helps users make more disciplined and informed investment decisions without surrendering control.

## 3. Initial persona

The initial persona is a private, sophisticated individual investor who values risk discipline, explainability, operator approval, and reliable Paper broker integration.

## 4. Future personas

Future personas may include experienced individual investors, active traders with stronger risk controls, family investment offices, small investment teams, advisers using decision-support tools, and users managing supported brokers. These segments require legal, regulatory, security, and product validation.

## 5. Core user journeys

1. Morning portfolio review.
2. Opportunity discovery.
3. Candidate investigation.
4. Risk assessment.
5. Trade-plan creation.
6. Operator approval.
7. Paper-order submission.
8. Broker acknowledgment.
9. Order-state monitoring.
10. Cancellation.
11. Fill reconciliation.
12. Position monitoring.
13. Exit planning.
14. Post-trade review.
15. Audit-trail review.
16. System failure or broker outage.
17. Secret or credential revocation.
18. Emergency stop.

## 6. Daily operating experience

A conceptual opening might say: Good morning, Eduardo. Markets are stable. Three opportunities meet your current criteria. Two existing positions require review. No immediate action is required.

Conceptual areas may include portfolio health, capital at risk, risk budget remaining, open positions, unresolved broker events, watchlist candidates, market conditions, important economic events, pending approvals, and system/data status. These are experience concepts, not guaranteed current features.

## 7. Dashboard principles

Dashboards should be calm, structured, factual, non-gamified, explicit about data status, and clear about simulation, Paper, or any future live mode.

## 8. Opportunity review experience

Opportunity review should show evidence, ranking rationale, data freshness, assumptions, and why no action may be appropriate.

## 9. Risk-review experience

Risk review should lead with downside, exposure, planned loss, risk budget, portfolio concentration, reward/risk, and policy rejections.

## 10. Trade-approval experience

Approval must be explicit, intentional, and tied to one material action. The interface must distinguish preview, approval, submission, acknowledgment, and final broker state.

## 11. Broker-status experience

Broker status must be unambiguous: acknowledged, filled, partially filled, rejected, cancelled, unresolved, stale, or unavailable.

## 12. Portfolio-monitoring experience

Portfolio monitoring should show current positions, exposure, risk budget, unresolved broker state, and changes that need review before suggesting new trades.

## 13. Alert and notification experience

Alerts should be useful, sparse, and grounded in real state changes. They should not manufacture urgency.

## 14. Failure and degraded-mode experience

Failures should be visible and actionable. Degraded mode should state what is unavailable, what remains safe, and what actions are blocked.

## 15. Mobile experience

Future mobile experience may support monitoring, approval, alerts, and emergency stop. It must not reduce confirmation quality or hide risk.

## 16. Accessibility

EMERS should use readable text, clear hierarchy, keyboard-aware flows, semantic status labels, color-independent risk states, and language that avoids unnecessary jargon.

## 17. Trust-building interactions

Trust is built by showing evidence, risk, limitations, broker truth, audit trails, confirmation steps, and honest uncertainty.

## 18. Prohibited experience patterns

Prohibited patterns include confetti, streaks, reward loops based on trading frequency, countdown pressure, fear-of-missing-out messaging, profit celebration without risk context, hidden unresolved orders, ambiguous broker status, and default approval of consequential actions.

## 19. Experience success measures

Measures include reduced confusion, fewer unsafe submissions, clear rejection understanding, successful reconciliation, fewer unresolved states, and user ability to explain why an action was or was not taken.

## 20. Open design questions

Open questions include visual identity, mobile approval depth, alert thresholds, accessibility standard, terminology for future live mode, and how much AI explanation is useful without creating false confidence.
